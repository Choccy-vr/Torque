## Resource collection

The most useful habit you can build is **searching things up**. There are *so* many resources out there, and this list only covers a few.

### Project inspiration
- [Adafruit Learn](https://learn.adafruit.com/guides/latest) has hundreds of projects, many with motors and full wiring guides.
- [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com) posts cool hardware hacks every day.
- [Printables](https://www.printables.com/) and [Thingiverse](https://www.thingiverse.com/) for mechanism ideas (for reference, not for copying; see the [guidelines](/docs/projects/project-guidelines)).

### Motor control
- [SimpleFOC](https://simplefoc.com/) is an open-source library for controlling BLDC and stepper motors with field-oriented control. Great for Level 3–4 builds.
- [AccelStepper](https://www.airspayce.com/mikem/arduino/AccelStepper/) is the go-to Arduino library for smooth stepper acceleration.
- [ODrive docs](https://docs.odriverobotics.com/) are a great read on high-performance BLDC control, even if you don't use an ODrive.

### Software

- **CAD:** [Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/) (browser, free for education) or Fusion 360.
- **PCB:** [KiCad](https://www.kicad.org/), free and open source, works everywhere.
- **Sketching:** [Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com/) for quick diagrams that still look good.

### 3D models & footprints
- [GrabCAD library](https://grabcad.com/library) has a model of basically any motor, bearing, or extrusion.
- [SnapEDA](https://www.snapeda.com/) for footprints and symbols (though making your own is a great skill).
- Most motor sellers publish STEP files: always grab the real model before designing a mount.

### Communities
First and foremost, the [Hack Club Slack](https://hackclub.com/slack/) and [#torque](https://hackclub.enterprise.slack.com/archives/C0BHGT33JHH). Beyond that:

- [KiCad Discord](https://www.kicad.org/community/chat/) for PCB help
- and more!
