Need a starting point? Here are a few motor build guides from around the web. They aren't the only way, or even always the best way, to build these projects. They're just a good place to see how the mechanisms work. Each card says which motors the guide uses.

Treat them as a first draft to improve on. Some choices in these guides are dated or overkill. For example, the door lock runs on a Raspberry Pi, where an ESP32 would be smaller, cheaper, and still have Wi-Fi built in.

If your idea isn't here, search for it. Google is your friend: almost every mechanism, part, or problem you can think of has a guide, forum thread, or video out there. Try searches like *"ESP32 stepper motor tutorial"*, *"how to mount a NEMA 17"*, or *"servo jitter fix"*.

<Callout type="warn" title="Make it your own">
  Torque doesn't accept direct clones, so build your own version instead of following a guide step by step. A few ways to do that:

  - **Customize the CAD.** Redesign the parts to fit your space, your motors, or a mechanism you came up with.
  - **Design a PCB.** Most of these guides stop at a wiring diagram and loose wires. A custom PCB makes your build cleaner and more reliable.
  - **Swap the parts.** Use a better microcontroller, a stronger motor, or a newer driver.
  - **Write your own firmware.** Add features the original doesn't have.

  Credit any guide you lean on in your README. See [Project Guidelines](/docs/projects/project-guidelines) for what counts as your own work.
</Callout>
