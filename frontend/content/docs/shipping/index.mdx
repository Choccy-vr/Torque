In short, **shipping is making your project shareable**. It's *the* most important step, almost as important as the project itself.

When you first make something, it usually just lives on your computer (and on your desk). Only you can see it. A few years from now, even *you* won't remember how it worked. **It's not real yet.**

Shipping means publishing your project so the world can see it, understand it, and rebuild it:

- **Document what it is:** the story behind it, what it does, how it all fits together, and lots of pictures.
- **Organize your files** so CAD, PCB, and firmware are easy to find.
- **Put it somewhere shareable**, like a public GitHub repo.

---

## When do you ship in Torque?

**You can ship more than once.** Every ship is a checkpoint where reviewers look at your journal and approve your hours.

| Path           | First ship                                         | Extra ships (as many as you need)                                                | Final ship                                                    |
| :------------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Funding**    | Your finished **design**, to get funding for parts | A **v2** (or v3...) design, if you need more funding to fix or improve something | Your finished, **working build**. Leftover hours become Volts |
| **No funding** | —                                                  | —                                                                                | Your finished, **working build**. All hours become Volts      |

<Callout type="idea" title="Need more funding for a v2?">
  Build hit a wall? Burned a driver, picked a motor that's too weak, or need a new PCB revision? Just **repeat the steps**: keep journaling and tracking time while you redesign, then ship again. The new hours can be claimed as another round of funding, as many times as your project needs, until you ship the finished, working build.
</Callout>

See [Paths](/docs/projects/paths) for the details.
